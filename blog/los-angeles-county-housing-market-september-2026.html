Los Angeles County Housing Market September 2026: Prices Rise as Sales Slow

By Paul Adams II · Market Analysis · October 28, 2026

September data from the California Association of Realtors shows the Los Angeles County housing market sending mixed signals: the median completed-sale price rose while annual transaction volume fell. Neither figure alone tells the full story.

What happened to home prices in September?

The California Association of Realtors reported a $994,670 median sale price for existing single-family detached homes in Los Angeles County in September 2026. That compares with $946,950 in August 2026 and $983,230 in September 2025, roughly 5% higher month over month and approximately 1.2% higher year over year. The median reflects the mix of homes that actually closed, not the appreciation of any particular property. For context on how this compares with the previous month, see the August 2026 Los Angeles market report.

Why are sales slowing while prices rise?

County single-family sales rose 5.4% from August but were 10.9% below September 2025. Higher financing costs, owners reluctant to surrender historically low mortgage rates and limited affordable inventory may each be constraining transaction volume. A decline in sales volume does not automatically mean prices are declining: when fewer but more premium homes sell, the median can rise even as overall market activity softens.

What are mortgage rates doing?

Freddie Mac reported an average 30-year fixed rate of 7.32% on October 22, 2026, compared with 7.40% the prior week and 6.19% a year earlier. A buyer's actual quote will depend on their specific finances, loan type and lender relationship. For a detailed look at how rate changes translate to monthly payments on a Los Angeles purchase, see the earlier analysis of mortgage rates and their impact on Los Angeles buyers. Even modest differences in rate can materially change what a given purchase costs per month.

What does city-level listing data show?

Realtor.com's September 2026 Los Angeles city report indicated approximately 20,130 active listings, up 4.2% year over year; a median asking price of approximately $1,034,500, down 5.9%; and price reductions on roughly 17.1% of listings. City-level asking-price data and county-level completed-sale medians measure different populations at different stages of the transaction. They must not be compared directly as though they represent the same statistic.

Are buyers gaining negotiating power?

Some are. Listings that have sat longer than comparable properties, undergone price reductions or require significant repairs are more likely to involve negotiation. Desirable, well-priced homes in strong neighborhoods can still attract competition even in this environment. Compare current competing listings and recent closed sales for the specific property type and location before deciding what to offer.

What should buyers do now?

Start with a complete monthly budget that includes property taxes, insurance, any HOA fees and a realistic maintenance reserve. Analyze the actual neighborhood and property type rather than relying on county-level averages. Ask why a listing has remained on the market: the explanation may be pricing, physical condition or financing restrictions that affect what types of loans the property qualifies for.

What should sellers do now?

Price against current competition and recent comparable closings in your specific area. A high county median is not a substitute for property-specific analysis. Accurate pricing, strong photography and photography, accessible showings and clear disclosure information help qualified buyers recognize value without extended negotiation delays.

What about condos and multifamily properties?

The CAR median cited here concerns existing detached single-family homes only. Condo analysis requires review of the HOA financials, reserve fund adequacy and project-level financing restrictions, which can affect which loan types buyers may use. For a guide to condo purchase financing and what to check before making an offer, see the condominium financing overview. Multifamily property analysis requires review of rents, operating expenses, local tenant protections, capital needs and appropriate financing structures. Neither asset class should be valued by simply applying the detached-home median.

Is Los Angeles a buyer's market?

Conditions are selective rather than uniform across the county. Some sellers are reducing prices; well-positioned listings in desirable locations still sell competitively. Location, condition, property type and asking price determine the practical balance of negotiating power for any given transaction.

Frequently asked questions

What was the September 2026 LA County median home price? $994,670 for existing detached single-family homes, per the California Association of Realtors.

Were sales higher than a year earlier? No. County single-family sales were down 10.9% compared with September 2025.

Are all home prices in Los Angeles rising? No. Different geographies, property types and measurements can show different trends simultaneously.

Should I wait until 2027 to buy? No one can guarantee future prices or rates. Base the decision on your affordability, time horizon and the specific property and neighborhood rather than on a prediction about market direction.

Paul's take

One headline cannot describe the whole Los Angeles market. Prices can rise while transaction volume falls, and asking prices can soften while completed-sale medians increase. When I evaluate a home for a client, I look at the comparable properties that actually compete with it: same property type, same neighborhood, same recent months. That is more useful than any county-level number.

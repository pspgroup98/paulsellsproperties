The Horror Show Opens at the Academy Museum: What to Know About LA's New Fall Exhibition

By Paul Adams II · Los Angeles Real Estate Advisor · September 30, 2026

Los Angeles has a major new fall exhibition, and it is particularly well suited to the city that built much of the mythology it explores.

The Horror Show opened September 26 at the Academy Museum of Motion Pictures in Miracle Mile. The large-scale exhibition explores more than a century of horror filmmaking through nearly 350 objects, immersive displays and six themed galleries. It remains on view through July 25, 2027.

For people who live in Los Angeles, this is more than a seasonal Halloween attraction. It is another reason to spend time along a stretch of Wilshire Boulevard that has accumulated one of the country's strongest concentrations of cultural institutions.

What is The Horror Show?

The Academy Museum describes the exhibition as an exploration of horror's cultural impact and enduring popularity.

Visitors move through six thematic areas focused on Gothic, Psychological, Science, Slasher, Religion and Ghosts. The galleries include original props, costumes, production materials and interactive elements.

That structure allows the exhibition to look beyond individual movies. Instead of simply displaying famous objects, it examines why certain ideas repeatedly frighten audiences and how filmmakers have reinvented those ideas across generations.

How large is the exhibition?

The Horror Show occupies the Academy Museum's fourth-floor Marilyn and Jeffrey Katzenberg Gallery. The museum says the exhibition contains nearly 350 original objects.

It is the museum's sixth major exhibition in that gallery, following exhibitions devoted to subjects including Hayao Miyazaki, Black cinema, John Waters, color in film and Jaws.

This is therefore not a small seasonal installation. It is one of the institution's major exhibition programs.

Is there programming beyond the exhibition?

Yes. The museum launched the exhibition alongside a John Carpenter retrospective.

Programming includes screenings of films such as Halloween, They Live and Assault on Precinct 13, with additional events continuing through the fall.

A Monster Mash is scheduled for October 24, Museum After Dark takes place October 31, and a 50th anniversary screening of Carrie with Sissy Spacek is scheduled for November 19. That programming gives the exhibition a longer life than opening weekend.

What is Zombies!?

The Academy Museum also opened Zombies!, a smaller companion exhibition in the adjacent Warner Bros. Gallery.

It explores how filmmakers create zombies and how zombie stories evolved into one of cinema's most recognizable horror traditions.

Unlike the primary exhibition, Zombies! is specifically designed to be family-friendly, although the museum still suggests parental guidance. It is included with museum admission.

Why does this matter to Miracle Mile?

The Academy Museum is part of a much larger cultural district.

Within a relatively short stretch of Wilshire and Exposition-area Los Angeles, residents can access LACMA, the Academy Museum, the Petersen Automotive Museum, La Brea Tar Pits and Museum, and other institutions.

That concentration matters because destinations reinforce one another. Someone may initially visit for a museum exhibition, then eat nearby, walk the neighborhood or return for another institution. That creates activity beyond the museum property itself.

The transportation picture has also changed

Miracle Mile became easier to reach without a car earlier this year when the first section of Metro's D Line Subway Extension opened.

Metro says Section 1 opened May 8, extending subway service west from Koreatown into the Wilshire/La Cienega area and serving Miracle Mile and surrounding neighborhoods.

The larger D Line project will eventually continue through Beverly Hills and Century City to Westwood.

For museums and cultural institutions, rail access matters. A destination that previously required visitors from many parts of Los Angeles to drive now has another way to attract people from across the region.

Does cultural investment affect residential real estate?

It can influence how people experience and perceive a neighborhood, but I would be careful about turning that into a simplistic property-value claim.

A museum does not automatically raise the value of every nearby home. Cultural institutions can increase neighborhood activity, support restaurants and businesses and create amenities residents value. They can also create traffic and event congestion.

For a buyer, the more relevant question is personal. Do you value being close to museums and urban amenities? Or would you rather be farther from major destinations and their associated activity?

Why Miracle Mile remains distinctive

Los Angeles is often described as decentralized. Miracle Mile is interesting because it functions differently.

A large collection of museums and cultural destinations sits along a recognizable corridor rather than being scattered across the region.

The Academy Museum added another major institution to that mix when it opened in 2021. Now the institution's changing exhibition calendar gives residents reasons to return even if they have already seen the permanent collection.

That recurring programming is important. A museum that people visit once has a different neighborhood relationship from one that continually creates new reasons to come back.

What else is changing around Wilshire?

Miracle Mile is also part of the ongoing transformation of Wilshire Boulevard.

The D Line expansion is changing regional access. Major cultural institutions continue investing in their campuses. New residential and commercial projects continue to reshape different portions of the corridor.

That combination of transportation, culture and real estate is why I think Wilshire is one of the more interesting corridors to watch in Los Angeles. The changes are not happening for one reason. Several different forms of investment are occurring simultaneously.

Is The Horror Show just for horror fans?

Probably not.

Someone deeply interested in horror will obviously get more from the subject matter, but the exhibition also deals with production design, costumes, makeup, filmmaking and the cultural history of movies.

That makes it relevant to people interested in Los Angeles' entertainment history even if horror is not their favorite genre. The Academy Museum is particularly well positioned for that type of exhibition because the institution can connect movie objects with the people and techniques that created them.

How long will The Horror Show be open?

Through July 25, 2027.

That means there is no need to treat this as a one-weekend event. The fall programming makes September and October especially timely, but the exhibition will remain available well into next year.

Frequently asked questions

When did The Horror Show open? September 26, 2026.

Where is it? At the Academy Museum of Motion Pictures in Los Angeles' Miracle Mile.

How long is the exhibition open? Through July 25, 2027.

What does the exhibition include? Nearly 350 objects, interactive displays and galleries organized around six horror themes.

Is there a family-friendly option? Yes. Zombies! is a smaller companion exhibition designed for visitors of all ages, although parental guidance is suggested.

Can you take Metro to the Academy Museum? The D Line now serves the Miracle Mile area following the May 2026 opening of the first extension section.

Paul's take

One thing I like about living in Los Angeles is that neighborhood amenities are not static.

A museum you have already visited can suddenly give you a reason to return. A subway opening can change how you get there. A new restaurant can change what you do before or afterward.

That combination matters when people decide where they actually want to live. The Horror Show is temporary, but it is part of a much larger story about Miracle Mile becoming an increasingly connected cultural district.

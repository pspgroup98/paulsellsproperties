West Hollywood Is Adding New Housing Along Santa Monica Boulevard: What Buyers and Owners Should Watch

By Paul Adams II · Los Angeles Real Estate Advisor · September 14, 2026

West Hollywood is in the middle of a visible housing and development cycle, particularly along and near Santa Monica Boulevard. Several projects are moving at once, including a substantial mixed-use building at Santa Monica Boulevard and Crescent Heights and the newly completed Marigold West affordable senior development near the corridor.

For buyers and existing homeowners, that does not automatically mean values are going up or down. What matters is how individual projects change housing supply, streets, retail activity, views, traffic and the experience of living on a particular block.

What is being built at 8025 Santa Monica Boulevard?

One of the most visible current projects is at 8025 Santa Monica Boulevard, at Crescent Heights. The seven-story mixed-use development is now rising above the site, with apartments, ground-floor commercial space and structured parking.

Recent project reporting describes 115 studio, one-bedroom and two-bedroom apartments. City approval records have reflected revisions during the entitlement process, so buyers evaluating a nearby property should focus on the current approved and permitted project rather than an old marketing summary.

That intersection matters because it sits along one of West Hollywood's principal commercial corridors and within walking distance of a substantial concentration of restaurants, shops, nightlife and housing. This is the type of project that can gradually change the character of a corridor without transforming an entire neighborhood overnight.

West Hollywood is adding housing in several forms

The larger story is not a single apartment building. On September 10, construction wrapped on Marigold West at 1041 N. Martel Avenue, just south of Santa Monica Boulevard. The five-story project contains 50 affordable studio, one-bedroom and two-bedroom homes reserved for residents 62 and older. Half are intended for people experiencing or at risk of homelessness.

Other infill projects are also moving through West Hollywood. The result is not one giant construction wave. It is a series of projects of very different sizes occurring across a relatively small city.

Does new apartment construction affect nearby condo values?

Potentially, but not in a simple way. New rental housing does not necessarily compete directly with an existing condominium. A buyer considering a two-bedroom condo may not view a new luxury apartment as a substitute for ownership. But development can still affect the surrounding market.

Mixed-use projects can add residents and commercial spaces to corridors that previously contained lower-density buildings or underused lots. More residents can support restaurants, grocery stores, fitness businesses and neighborhood services. That can improve daily convenience.

For a condo owner immediately adjacent to new construction, the impact can be different. A new multi-story building may affect views, natural light or privacy. That is why buyers should look beyond the unit itself and consider what could potentially happen on nearby parcels.

Development also brings temporary disruption. Noise, trucks, lane closures and construction activity can make a block less pleasant even when the completed project eventually improves the corridor. Repeated projects can also add meaningfully to the city's housing stock over time.

Why is Santa Monica Boulevard attracting development?

Santa Monica Boulevard already functions as West Hollywood's major east-west commercial spine. It offers a concentration of restaurants, nightlife, retail, services and employment within a city where many residents value being able to walk for at least some daily needs.

That makes sites along the corridor logical locations for denser residential and mixed-use construction. It is also worth remembering that West Hollywood is its own incorporated city. A project in West Hollywood is governed by West Hollywood land-use rules rather than City of Los Angeles zoning, even though traveling a few blocks can put you back inside Los Angeles.

Should buyers be concerned about development near a property?

They should be informed, not automatically concerned. If you are considering buying a condo and there is a one-story commercial building directly across the street, I would not assume that building will stay one story forever.

Look at the surrounding parcels. Ask whether there are active entitlement applications. Look at current construction. Consider whether an adjacent property appears underdeveloped relative to the corridor. None of that means a project will definitely happen. It means the buyer is evaluating the surrounding real estate rather than treating the current view as permanent.

Could more housing improve the neighborhood?

It can. Additional residents create customers for local businesses. New projects may replace vacant, aging or low-intensity commercial properties with buildings that put more people directly on the corridor. Ground-floor commercial space can also introduce new restaurants and services.

But good neighborhood development depends on more than adding units. Architecture, sidewalk design, parking access, landscaping, ground-floor uses and the relationship of the building to surrounding homes all affect whether a project improves the pedestrian experience.

What does this mean for West Hollywood sellers?

For most sellers, the useful approach is explaining the property's actual context rather than making broad claims about redevelopment. Is the condo two blocks from a growing concentration of restaurants? Is a long-vacant site nearby becoming housing? Will a particular development add retail? Could construction temporarily affect access? Does the unit have a protected orientation, or could another building eventually rise nearby?

Those are useful details because they help a buyer understand the property as part of a changing neighborhood.

What should investors watch?

West Hollywood development can provide information about where capital and developers see demand. But investors should not infer that every property near new construction is automatically undervalued. Look at actual rents, vacancy, regulations, acquisition price and operating expenses.

Also distinguish between West Hollywood and the City of Los Angeles because rental regulations differ across municipal boundaries.

Frequently asked questions

What is being built at 8025 Santa Monica Boulevard? A seven-story mixed-use apartment project with ground-floor commercial space is under construction at Santa Monica Boulevard and Crescent Heights.

Is West Hollywood adding affordable housing? Yes. Marigold West recently completed 50 affordable senior apartments near Santa Monica Boulevard.

Does new construction lower existing home values? Not necessarily. Effects can vary based on property type, proximity, views, amenities, noise, streetscape improvements and the quality of the finished development.

Should condo buyers research nearby development? Yes. Understanding active projects and potentially developable neighboring sites is a useful part of neighborhood due diligence.

Paul's take

West Hollywood is already a built-out, highly desirable city, which means change tends to happen parcel by parcel rather than through giant new subdivisions. That makes individual development sites more important.

When I evaluate a West Hollywood property, I want to understand not only what the block looks like today, but what is being built around it and how that could change the experience of owning there.

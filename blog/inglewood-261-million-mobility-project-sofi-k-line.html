Inglewood's $261 Million Mobility Overhaul: What It Could Mean for Downtown, SoFi Stadium and the K Line

By Paul Adams II · Los Angeles Real Estate Advisor · September 9, 2026

Inglewood is moving ahead with a $261 million transportation and public-realm project designed to improve connections among the Metro K Line, Downtown Inglewood, SoFi Stadium, Kia Forum and Intuit Dome.

For people thinking about living, buying, investing, or operating property in Inglewood, the important story is bigger than getting people to Olympic events. It is about whether the city can better connect its growing entertainment district with the neighborhoods and commercial streets around it.

What is Inglewood building?

In late August, the Inglewood City Council approved a public-private partnership to deliver transportation and streetscape improvements intended to improve access between major destinations in the city.

The partnership will build mobility hubs and rework portions of Downtown Inglewood's streetscape. Current project information places the investment at roughly $261 million, with construction beginning this fall and roadway work expected to start in November.

The improvements are intended to serve everyday travel as well as the more than 60 major annual events hosted at the city's sports and entertainment venues and transportation demands surrounding the 2028 Olympic Games.

Why does this matter beyond SoFi Stadium?

Inglewood has spent years attracting destination-scale investment. SoFi Stadium opened in 2020. Intuit Dome followed in 2024. Kia Forum was already one of the region's major entertainment venues.

The challenge is that a collection of major destinations is not automatically the same thing as an integrated neighborhood.

For residents, the more important questions are practical. Can you comfortably get around without driving for every trip? Can a visitor arrive by transit and easily reach restaurants, businesses and events? Can Downtown Inglewood benefit from traffic generated by the stadium district? Are streets comfortable enough to walk?

Transportation and public-realm improvements matter because they affect those questions.

What is the role of the K Line?

Metro's K Line already provides rail service through Inglewood and connects into the broader Los Angeles rail network.

The long-term value of rail infrastructure depends partly on what happens after a rider exits the station. A train can bring someone close to a destination, but the usefulness of the trip changes if the final portion requires a difficult walk, a rideshare, or a confusing transfer.

That is why first-mile and last-mile infrastructure receives so much attention in modern transit planning. Improved pedestrian routes, transit connections, wayfinding, public spaces and streetscapes can make existing rail infrastructure substantially more useful without building another rail line.

Could this affect real estate values?

Potentially, but the relationship should not be oversimplified. Real estate advertising sometimes implies that any new transit investment automatically raises nearby property values. That is not how I would analyze it.

The effect on an individual property depends on exact distance to new improvements, ease of access, noise and traffic, zoning, property type, neighborhood amenities, parking, development potential, commercial activity, construction impacts, and the quality of the finished public realm.

A house on a quiet residential block may respond differently from a mixed-use parcel near Downtown Inglewood. A small apartment building may have a different investment thesis than a single-family home. The infrastructure is one input.

Why Downtown Inglewood may be particularly important

The biggest long-term opportunity may be the relationship between the historic downtown and the newer venue district.

Large venues bring enormous numbers of people to Inglewood. If transportation and pedestrian improvements make it easier for those visitors to patronize local restaurants, shops and businesses before and after events, more of the economic activity could potentially spread beyond venue walls.

That can help determine whether a major entertainment investment behaves like an isolated island or becomes a stronger anchor for surrounding districts. The distinction matters to commercial landlords, residential property owners and people evaluating the neighborhood as a place to live.

What about the 2028 Olympics?

Olympics preparation is clearly accelerating transportation investment throughout Los Angeles County. The key question for property owners is what remains after 2028.

A temporary shuttle for an Olympic event has little long-term real-estate significance. A permanently improved sidewalk, intersection, transit connection, streetscape or mobility hub can affect daily neighborhood function for years.

That is why the lasting infrastructure matters more than the event itself.

What should an Inglewood buyer watch?

Pay attention to the exact location of the improvements rather than simply saying the property is near SoFi. Consider realistic walking routes to Downtown Inglewood, a K Line station, or the venue district.

Major public projects create temporary disruption before they create benefits. Infrastructure investment can encourage new development, but what can actually be built still depends on parcel-level land-use rules.

Living near major venues can be convenient, but event days can change traffic patterns. Watch which corridors gain restaurants, retail and neighborhood-serving businesses, not only destination entertainment.

Why this belongs in a real-estate conversation

People do not experience Los Angeles only from inside their houses. Transportation, restaurants, sidewalks, grocery stores, parks, workplaces and entertainment all affect how a neighborhood functions.

Infrastructure can also change which areas feel connected to one another. Inglewood is a particularly interesting example because several large investments have happened relatively quickly. The next phase is about connection.

Frequently asked questions

How much is Inglewood's new mobility project? Current project information places the investment at approximately $261 million.

What will it connect? The project is intended to improve transportation and public-realm connections involving the K Line, SoFi Stadium, Kia Forum, Intuit Dome and Downtown Inglewood.

When will construction begin? Construction is scheduled to begin in fall 2026, with roadway work expected to start in November.

Is the project only for the Olympics? No. The 2028 Games are an important catalyst, but the infrastructure is intended to support local travel and major events beyond the Olympics.

Paul's take

Inglewood is one of the clearest examples of why I think real-estate analysis should include what is happening outside the property line.

A stadium is one thing. A stadium, rail station, downtown district, restaurants, pedestrian network and neighborhood that increasingly function together is something different. The next several years will tell us how successfully those pieces connect.

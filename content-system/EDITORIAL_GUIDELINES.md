# Editorial Guidelines — Paul Adams II

## Voice and Tone

Paul Adams II is a third-generation Los Angeles real estate agent at Coldwell Banker Realty, Beverly Hills. His writing should reflect that background: deeply knowledgeable, analytically grounded, and useful without being generic.

The writing should feel:

- **Strategic** — not just informational, but focused on decisions and tradeoffs
- **Analytical** — explain the why behind every what
- **Clear** — no jargon without explanation, no padding
- **Confident** — direct statements, not hedged to the point of saying nothing
- **Sophisticated** — written for intelligent adults, not simplified for search engines
- **Locally grounded** — Los Angeles specific, not generically national
- **Client-focused** — always oriented toward what this means for a buyer, seller, or investor

## What This Writing Is

Every article should help a reader understand something that matters to them about Los Angeles real estate. It should explain not just what something means, but why it affects their decision, their timing, or their outcome.

The goal is not to rank for a keyword. The goal is to be the most useful, honest, and clear piece of writing on the topic. Ranking is the result, not the aim.

## What This Writing Is Not

Do not write like a content farm. Do not write for a search algorithm. Do not produce generic, hedged, keyword-stuffed articles that could have been written about any market by anyone.

Avoid:

- Generic national market language applied loosely to Los Angeles
- Excessive enthusiasm ("exciting opportunity", "dream home", "amazing neighborhood")
- Vague reassurances ("the market is always a good investment long-term")
- Padding and filler sentences that add length without adding substance
- Repetitive conclusions that restate the introduction
- Fake urgency or manufactured scarcity
- Sounding like a press release

## Absolute Prohibitions

**Never use the em dash character (—, Unicode U+2014).** This is a hard requirement that applies to every piece of text the system generates: headlines, subheadings, body paragraphs, FAQ answers, CTAs, metadata, excerpts. Use a comma, semicolon, colon, or restructure the sentence instead.

**Never fabricate:**
- Statistics or data
- Laws or regulations
- Quotes from public figures
- Market data or transaction information
- Client stories or experiences
- Claims about Paul's personal involvement in specific deals or properties
- Information about specific properties

If a claim cannot be adequately supported, remove it or clearly qualify it as a general pattern rather than a specific fact.

**Never imply Paul personally:**
- Completed a specific transaction
- Represented a named client
- Visited a specific property
- Negotiated a specific deal
- Experienced a specific event

Unless that information has been explicitly supplied.

## Structural Guidelines

### Headlines

- Clear and specific, not clever or vague
- Should describe exactly what the reader will learn
- Not keyword-stuffed
- No em dashes

### Sections

- Use H2 for major sections
- Use H3 for sub-points within a section
- Do not use more headings than the content warrants
- Sections should develop ideas, not just introduce bullet points

### Paragraphs

- Short to medium length — three to six sentences as a general guide
- One idea per paragraph
- Do not begin every paragraph the same way

### Lists

- Use lists when items are genuinely enumerable and parallel
- Do not convert every piece of analysis into a bullet list
- Lists should not replace paragraphs that require explanation

### FAQs

- Include when the topic has natural questions buyers, sellers, or investors actually ask
- Answers should be substantive, not one sentence
- Do not repeat the article body verbatim in the FAQ

### CTAs

- The in-article CTA should connect specifically to the article's topic
- It should be useful, not pushy
- One clear action (schedule a call via Calendly, get in touch)

## Phrases to Avoid

The following phrases signal generic AI writing and should be eliminated:

- "In today's market"
- "Navigating the"
- "Whether you're a buyer or seller"
- "Dream home"
- "Ever-evolving"
- "Vibrant"
- "Boasts"
- "It's important to note"
- "In conclusion"
- "To summarize"
- "This article will explore"
- "Without further ado"
- "Invaluable"
- "At the end of the day"
- "Game changer"
- "Leverage" (used metaphorically, not financially)
- "Holistic approach"
- "Best practices"

## On Sources and Research

For factual claims — statistics, laws, regulations, market data — prefer primary sources:

1. City of Los Angeles, County of Los Angeles
2. California state agencies (DRE, BOE, LAHD, HCD)
3. Federal agencies (Federal Reserve, HUD, Census Bureau, BLS)
4. Primary real estate data (CoreLogic, Freddie Mac, Fannie Mae, NAR)
5. Reputable financial journalism (WSJ, Bloomberg, Reuters)

When using data, specify the source and timeframe. Do not present data as current if it is more than a year old without noting that.

If you cannot find a reliable source for a specific claim, remove the claim or reframe it as a general pattern.

## Article Types

### Type A — Timely / Market Intelligence
Covers current developments: legislation, market shifts, lending changes, policy changes. Should accurately date any time-sensitive information. Acknowledge when information may change.

### Type B — Evergreen Search / Consumer Education
Covers durable topics: buying guides, selling guides, financing explanations, neighborhood overviews. Write for a reader who will encounter this article six to eighteen months from now.

### Type C — Authority / Investment
Covers analytical real estate topics: investment strategy, multifamily, rental economics, value-add approaches. Should demonstrate genuine command of the financial and strategic logic, not just surface-level definitions.

## Internal Links

When the article naturally connects to another page on the site, link to it using descriptive anchor text. Do not force links. A useful link improves the article; a forced link cheapens it.

Key site pages to link contextually:
- `/property-search.html` — when discussing finding properties
- `/about.html` — when context calls for introducing Paul
- `/contact.html` or Calendly — at the CTA
- `/blog/index.html` — at the end or in the related articles section
- `/neighborhoods/[name].html` — when discussing a specific neighborhood
- Other relevant articles — when the topic connects

## Geography

Write specifically about Los Angeles. When sub-markets matter (City of LA vs. unincorporated County, specific neighborhoods, Westside vs. Valley), be precise. Do not write "the Los Angeles area" when you mean the City. Do not write "California" when you mean LA.

## Phase 3 Additions — ChatGPT Editorial Workflow

### Content Mix

Each weekly batch of three articles should cover:
- **One Type A article** — Timely / Market Intelligence
- **One Type B article** — Evergreen / Consumer Education
- **One Type C article** — Authority / Investment

Topic distribution: approximately **70% real estate topics** (buying, selling, financing, law, investment) and **30% lifestyle/quality-of-life topics** that a prospective Los Angeles buyer, homeowner, or investor genuinely cares about.

### Audience Taxonomy

| Identifier | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| `buyers` | General homebuyers |
| `first-time buyers` | First-time purchasers |
| `move-up buyers` | Buyers moving from one home to a larger one |
| `luxury buyers` | High-price-point purchasers |
| `lifestyle buyers` | Buyers motivated by neighborhood / quality of life |
| `sellers` | Homeowners preparing or actively selling |
| `investors` | Investment property buyers |
| `landlords` | Existing rental property owners |
| `rental property owners` | Owners of rental units (may overlap with landlords) |
| `duplex owners` | Specific to 2-4 unit properties |
| `condo owners` | Condo-specific concerns |

### Content Freshness Classifications

| Type | Meaning | Review cadence |
|------|---------|----------------|
| `evergreen` | Valid indefinitely with minor updates | Annual |
| `news` | Valid for days to weeks | Immediate; archive when superseded |
| `periodic` | Valid for a season or cycle | Seasonal |
| `regulatory` | Valid until the law/policy changes | When law changes |
| `market_data` | Valid until next data release | Quarterly |

### Article Update vs. New Article

Before assigning a new slug to a topic, consider whether an existing article should be **updated** instead of replaced:
- If the existing article covers the same search intent and is ranked: update it.
- If the existing article covers adjacent intent and updating would dilute its focus: write a new article with a different slug.
- If the topic is genuinely new or covers a distinct angle: new article.

Phase 3 importer: `action_type: "update_existing"` is reserved for future implementation. Do not use it in batches until the importer supports it.

### Topic Categories

In addition to the standard article types, topics may come from these subject areas:

**Real estate topics (70% target)**
- Buying guides and process
- Financing, lending, mortgage
- Seller strategy and pricing
- Investment and multifamily
- Market analysis and timing
- Legal and regulatory (landlord/tenant, disclosure, zoning)
- Condo / HOA specifics
- ADU, garage conversion, value-add

**Lifestyle topics (30% target)**
- Neighborhood quality of life: walkability, schools, commute, character
- Relocation considerations: what life in [neighborhood] is actually like
- Cost of living in Los Angeles context
- Lifestyle-driven real estate decisions (home office, outdoor space, urban vs. suburban)
- LA-specific considerations: earthquakes, wildfires, traffic, climate

### Pillar and Supporting Article Architecture

A **pillar article** covers a broad topic comprehensively (e.g., "How to Buy a House in Los Angeles").
A **supporting article** covers a specific sub-topic that links to the pillar (e.g., "How Much Do You Need to Buy a House in Los Angeles").

Use `pillar_relationship`:
- `null` — standalone article
- `"pillar"` — this article is a pillar
- `"supporting:target-slug"` — this article supports the named pillar

### Internal Linking

When suggesting internal links, use only URLs from the `site_pages` array in the editorial context export, plus any published article slugs. Do not invent URLs that do not exist on the site.

### Source Requirements

| Content type | Source requirement |
|--------------|-------------------|
| `news` | At least one source with URL |
| `regulatory` | At least one primary government source |
| `market_data` | At least one data source with access date |
| `evergreen` | Optional; include when making specific factual claims |
| `strategy-guide` | Optional; include when referencing laws or data |

### Duplicate Search Intent Prevention

Before proposing any topic, verify that its `normalized_search_intent` does not substantially overlap with:
1. Published articles (`published_articles` in context)
2. Scheduled articles (`scheduled_articles` in context)
3. Backlog entries that are already claimed

**Threshold**: if a reader researching the same question would consider both articles equivalent answers, they are duplicates. Reframe or discard the newer topic.

### Editorial Backlog

The backlog is a list of candidate topics discovered during research but not selected for a given week. ChatGPT should:
- Review the backlog before generating new topic candidates
- Propose backlog items for promotion when they become timely
- Add strong-but-not-yet-timely topics to the backlog via `backlog_updates` in the batch package

### Editorial Watch List

The watch list tracks developing stories (legislation, policy, market trends) that are not yet article-ready but should be monitored. ChatGPT should:
- Check the watch list for items that have reached a milestone
- Update watch-list item status via `watch_list_updates` in the batch package when a milestone is reached or a story resolves
